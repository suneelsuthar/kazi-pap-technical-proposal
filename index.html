Kazi Pap! is designed as a two-sided mobile platform that connects young workers with customers who need fast services, errands, skilled work, and product access. Feature breakdown

What the platform needs to become usable, trusted, and fundable.

The MVP should avoid vanity features and focus on features that prove demand, supply, service completion, and money movement.

Supply side: youth workers

  • Phone number registration and OTP verification
  • Worker profile with photo, location, bio, skills, and availability
  • Service categories for skilled, semi-skilled, and quick tasks
  • Nearby job feed with pay, distance, urgency, and category filters
  • Accept, decline, or request clarification before confirming work
  • Job progress updates: accepted, on the way, started, completed
  • Wallet balance, payout history, and earning summary
  • Ratings and profile reputation

Demand side: customers/businesses

  • Customer signup with phone number and basic profile
  • Post task with category, description, budget, urgency, and location
  • Instant worker suggestions based on skill, proximity, and availability
  • Worker profile preview with rating, price, and status
  • Booking confirmation and live job status tracking
  • In-app communication for job coordination
  • Payment confirmation and completion approval
  • Ratings, reviews, and rehire flow

Admin and operations

  • Dashboard for total users, active jobs, completed jobs, and revenue
  • User management for workers and customers
  • Worker verification review and approval workflow
  • Job monitoring with status, timestamps, and cancellation visibility
  • Category and service management
  • Payment and payout monitoring
  • Dispute and support ticket tracking
  • Basic moderation for reviews and product listings

Growth modules

  • Micro-learning modules for worker upskilling
  • Product selling for crafts, food, and second-hand items
  • Referral rewards for worker/customer acquisition
  • Gamified badges for completed jobs and high ratings
  • Low-data mode optimization for basic smartphones
  • Swahili and English interface support
  • Emergency/help button and safety reporting
  • Community groups by category or city

Creative scope options

Three ways to move forward based on budget, risk, and launch ambition.

Each option is structured to protect the core business loop while giving the client a clear reason to choose the right level of investment.

Lean pilot

Trimmed-down MVP

$2,200starter build

Best for validating the core concept with a small controlled user group before committing to the full operational platform.

  • Worker and customer onboarding
  • Basic worker profiles and categories
  • Customer job posting flow
  • Manual or simplified matching
  • Basic job status tracking
  • Simple ratings and reviews
  • Basic admin dashboard
  • Payment flow prepared structurally, with limited or manual handling depending on API readiness

Ideal if the client wants a fast proof-of-concept and can accept operational workarounds during the first pilot.

Recommended

Full launch-focused MVP

$5,000complete MVP

Best for a more serious launch where the client needs marketplace credibility, stronger automation, admin visibility, and payment confidence.

  • Complete worker and customer mobile app flows
  • Phone OTP authentication
  • Worker verification-ready profile structure
  • Job posting, nearby job feed, and matching logic
  • Booking confirmation and progress tracking
  • In-app chat or structured messaging
  • Wallet, payment, and payout flow integration planning
  • Mobile money integration subject to provider/API access
  • Push notifications for key events
  • Admin panel for users, jobs, payments, disputes, and categories
  • Ratings, reviews, and basic reporting
  • QA, deployment support, and launch handover

This is the stronger recommendation because Kazi Pap! depends heavily on trust, payment visibility, and operational control.

Flexible path

Phased build

Milestonebased

Best if the client wants to start confidently without carrying the full launch budget immediately.

  • Phase 1: Discovery, UX, architecture, and clickable prototype
  • Phase 2: Core marketplace MVP
  • Phase 3: Payments, wallet, admin, and launch readiness
  • Phase 4: Product selling, learning modules, referrals, and growth features
  • Each phase produces a usable asset instead of a mysterious development cave

This keeps momentum high while reducing decision pressure for the client.

Phased approach

Start with the marketplace engine, then add the super-app layers.

This approach helps the client avoid overbuilding before the market validates the core earning and hiring loop.

Phase 1 UX, architecture, clickable prototype, and technical plan.
Phase 2 Worker/customer app, job posting, matching, tracking, admin basics.
Phase 3 Payments, wallet, mobile money, stronger admin, QA, launch support.

Suggested milestone structure

  • Kickoff milestone: finalize requirements, UX flow, technical architecture, data model, and delivery plan.
  • Core build milestone: implement auth, profiles, jobs, matching, booking, tracking, notifications, and basic admin.
  • Launch milestone: integrate payments, test mobile money flows, resolve bugs, deploy, and hand over.
  • Growth milestone: add product selling, micro-learning, referrals, gamification, and community features.

This structure gives the client something valuable at each step instead of asking them to fund the entire dream in one gulp, which history suggests humans rarely enjoy.

Timeline

Realistic delivery window with two developers using AI-assisted development.

AI tools can speed up implementation, but they do not remove product decisions, integrations, QA, provider approvals, and payment testing. Sadly, reality remains installed by default.

Week 1

Technical discovery and UX planning

Confirm final scope, user roles, payment flow, admin needs, data model, and priority screens.

Weeks 2–3

Design system and core screens

Design onboarding, worker profile, customer job posting, job feed, booking, tracking, wallet, ratings, and admin structure.

Weeks 3–6

Mobile app and backend foundation

Build authentication, users, profiles, categories, jobs, booking logic, storage, and core APIs.

Weeks 6–9

Marketplace logic, tracking, chat, and notifications

Implement matching, job lifecycle, progress updates, push notifications, messaging, ratings, and admin monitoring.

Weeks 9–12

Payments, wallet, QA, and deployment

Handle payment integration, payout flow, test cases, bug fixing, staging/production deployment, and client handover.

Estimated timeline: 8–10 weeks for a trimmed pilot, 10–14 weeks for a strong MVP, and 14–18 weeks if payment integration, admin depth, and QA need to be production-ready from day one.
Assumptions

Important notes before pricing gets treated like magic.

These assumptions keep the proposal fair, realistic, and protected against scope drift disguised as “just one small thing.”

Client responsibilities

  • Provide brand assets, content, policy text, and final business rules
  • Arrange access to mobile money provider APIs and sandbox credentials
  • Provide test users and feedback within agreed review windows

Scope boundaries

  • Advanced fraud detection is not part of the first MVP
  • Full call masking and emergency dispatch workflows can be added later
  • AI matching and advanced analytics are best suited for phase two or three

Delivery focus

  • Prioritize the core earning and hiring loop first
  • Keep the first release lightweight for Tanzanian mobile users
  • Build admin visibility early to support trust and operations
Recommendation

The best commercial path is the full MVP, with growth features phased after validation.

The $2,200 option is useful for a controlled pilot, but the $5,000 version better matches the seriousness of a two-sided marketplace with payments and trust requirements.

We recommend launching with the full MVP if the goal is to onboard real users, process jobs confidently, and present Kazi Pap! as a credible youth employment platform. If the client wants to reduce upfront risk, the phased approach can begin with UX, architecture, and a controlled pilot, then expand into mobile money, admin depth, product selling, and upskilling.
